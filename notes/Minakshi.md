Sanskrit Term for 'Moha'
According to the search results, “Moha” (मोह) is a Sanskrit word that translates to “illusion”, “delusion”, “darkness”, or “error”. It refers to a state of being misled or deceived, often characterized by ignorance or lack of understanding of the true nature of reality.
In various Sanskrit texts and traditions, Moha is described as:
	•	One of the six enemies (Ṣad Ūrmi) that hinder spiritual progress, along with Kāma (desire), Krodha (anger), Lobha (greed), Mada (intoxication), and Kāma (lust).
	•	A state of bewilderment or confusion, born of ignorance (moha-jam) or produced from illusory knowledge (moha-jam).
	•	A covering or knot of illusion (moha-paṭalā, moha-pāśaḥ) that needs to be overcome for spiritual liberation.
	•	One of the fivefold avidyā (ignorance), along with tamas, mahāmoha, tāmisra, and andhatāmisra.
	•	A kleśa (pain) or affliction of the jīva (individual self), along with other kleśas such as raga (greed), dveṣa (aversion), and avidyā (ignorance).
In the context of yoga and spirituality, Moha is seen as an obstacle to be overcome through practices such as meditation, self-reflection, and the cultivation of wisdom (prajñā). By recognizing and transcending Moha, one can gain a deeper understanding of reality and achieve spiritual liberation.
