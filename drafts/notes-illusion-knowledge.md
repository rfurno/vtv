Illusion of Knowledge
AI can produce impressive summaries by mashing up the entire internet. It often sounds authoritative, but it’s still just recombining existing information.

In contrast, what I’ve been receiving in the long-term Vedanta course with Acharya Jonas is something different. It’s not the accumulation of interesting facts or clever explanations. It's not a venn diagram or charts with comparisons of different perspectives.

It’s a slow, step-by-step process of being led to see for myself. Actually, I should say to experiment by myself. To try. To question. To challenge. Disagree but finally settling with something that is mine: my own understanding after having bringing that knowledge into my daily life.

For example, when I asked about the rope/snake analogy, an AI summary told me: “The rope is less real than the snake, but still not ultimately real — only Brahman is.” It sounded sophisticated, but it completely missed the point.

What I actually learned in class - emphasis on "I" - was that the entire framework of “reality,” “lesser reality,” and “ultimate reality” was being gently taken apart. There was no room left to hold onto those distinctions as solid concepts. I wasn’t collecting knowledge — I was being guided to see through the very way I was constructing knowledge in the first place.
That difference feels important because it is at the core of my current challenges: how do I let all dissolve, all emotions, all preconceptions, and experience a silence. A silence that Acharya alluded will be at the end of this journey of knowledge.
