**"We Were Never Cleaning the School. The School Was Cleaning Us."**

That Japanese *souji* ritual — kids sweeping and wiping floors every afternoon, no janitors — isn't really about clean tiles. The school was quietly shaping the kids through small, daily care.

When I started Ving Tsun, they told me to clean the Mo Kwoon and wash Sifu’s car. It was part of the teaching, but it felt bolted on. I did it, yet didn’t internalize it.

Years later, the real practice landed: the slow, repeated movements (visualizing palms turning forward, then letting the body follow). That’s **Kung Fu Life** — ordinary acts that reshape you from the inside.

**Sadhana** hits the same spot for me: showing up on time for class, studying mantras and ancient languages, staying present on camera. It displaces the dust — the news binges and doomscrolling — one disciplined session at a time.

Japan built this into school days. We can build it into ours. The floor (or the form, or the mind) gets cleaner as a side effect.
