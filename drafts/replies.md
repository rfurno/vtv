# Restack https://substack.com/inbox/post/199358650

Este artigo sobre Ahimsa me fez pensar no exemplo mais poderoso que conheço de evitar a violência contra si mesma: Yim Ving Tsun, fundadora do sistema Ving Tsun, aprendeu arte marcial justamente para escapar de um casamento forçado.

Parece paradoxal — usar algo que carrega a ideia de violência para evitar a violência —, mas talvez seja uma das formas mais profundas de Ahimsa: proteger a própria integridade quando o mundo tenta te forçar a algo que viola quem você é. Um lembrete de que, às vezes, o caminho da não-violência exige treino, intenção e coragem.


# Restack: https://substack.com/inbox/post/199454725
"You should be doing more. You should know what comes next. You should have figured it out already."

Angie’s piece made me examine the “why” behind all those shoulds. After asking why several times, I almost always reach the same roots: safety, pleasure, and comfort.

The real work is learning to see them clearly through discernment — what Vedanta calls viveka — and still choosing differently. A lifelong practice.


# Restack: https://substack.com/@ruthgaskovski/note/c-266125762

"Be aware of the triggers that want to pull you away into a dark or unhealthy place, and learn to replace those triggers with other actions that take you to a higher place."

Ruth and Peco’s note is a quiet masterclass. 

The real art isn’t just resisting the trigger — it’s catching it early and choosing a better direction. Through Vedanta and Ving Tsun, I’ve come to see this as a form of inner martial practice: you don’t fight the impulse with force, you discard the unhelpful with discernment.

And writing every day turns it into a lifelong skill.
