# “I stopped looking for the right place.”

I used to think real happiness would only come when I was *there* — same city as my family, same table, same country. The right place, the right time, the perfect setup.

Then one early spring day I was walking through the woods near my house. Flowers were blossoming everywhere, and it felt too good not to share. So I called my family abroad, out of nowhere. “Hey, you have to see this.”

We stayed on the line while I kept walking. I laughed at my total lack of knowledge about the flowers, and for those ten minutes the distance disappeared. I wasn’t waiting to go “back home.” Home was right there on the phone with me, five minutes from my house.

Another time I tried a new recipe that reminded me of my mom’s cooking. I called her on FaceTime and let her supervise from thousands of miles away. She completely changed the recipe, telling stories about her thousand variations. I went with the flow — and still managed to overcook it. (I didn’t tell her.)

It was simple, imperfect, and completely joyful.

I had been searching for happiness in the wrong direction. It isn’t waiting in another country or on the next vacation. It’s here, in ordinary moments, when I choose to connect instead of waiting. I still miss being there in person. But I’m done putting my happiness on hold until the perfect conditions line up.
