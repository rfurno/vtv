# The Lost Match
## All I Wanted Was to Play Futebol

I have a picture from when I was nine. Not a photograph. A picture I can still relive.

Recess. A bench at the edge of the concrete yard the school used as a futebol pitch. A small notebook on my knee. The other boys already choosing sides.

The job had started in a living room. My mother, her friend, and I. I barely remember the room. My mom told me that the friend could see things the rest of us could not. She told me I was going to be special. Or I had decided that she had. I would make important discoveries. I do not remember the sentence. I remember the job. I don't remember my mother's face in that conversation. I remember the second job: make my mom proud.

So I stayed on the bench and tried to come up with the ideia genial, the idea that would prove it. I held the pencil and waited for it to arrive.

I scribbled things that hardly fit together. Words looking for logic. Logic a nine-year-old did not have.

The idea did not come. The whistle did. Recess was over. I had not played.

# Bearings Lost

On my way to class, I'd stop by a well known book store in São Paulo, "Livraria Cultura", renowned for a great variety of technical books from abroad. I'd pick the same book on fluid mechanics and read a little. It was expensive for my non-existent budget. But looking back I now realize it was out of my league. Perhaps the Math wasn't that difficult but I was lost. For one I had this tremendous opportunity of studying at a great University. But my job of doing something genial, doing something special, was getting in the way of actually studying.
