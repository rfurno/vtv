# The Lost Match
## All I Wanted Was to Play Futebol

I have a picture from when I was nine. Not a photograph. A picture I can still relive.

Recess. A bench at the edge of the concrete yard the school used as a futebol pitch. A small notebook on my knee. The other boys already choosing sides.

The job had started in a living room. My mother, her friend, and I. I barely remember the room. My mom told me that the friend could see things the rest of us could not. This friend told me I was going to be special. Or I had decided that she had. I would make important discoveries. I do not remember the sentence. I remember the job. I don't remember my mother's face in that conversation. I remember the second job: make my mom proud.

So I stayed on the bench and tried to come up with the ideia genial, the idea that would prove it. I held the pencil and waited for it to arrive.

I scribbled things that hardly fit together. Words looking for logic. Logic a nine-year-old did not have.

The idea did not come. The whistle did. Recess was over. I had not played.

# Rumo Perdido

On the way to class I would stop at Livraria Cultura. Same book every time, a fluid mechanics text from abroad. I read a few pages. It cost more than I had.

Nobody was telling me to be special anymore. I was telling myself. The math was probably not the hard part. I was lost. I had the place at university, and the job was still running. It was getting in the way of studying the thing in front of me.

Perhaps it was arrogance. Ignorance would have been simpler. Arrogance got in the way of the assignments. It wanted leaps. I was barely walking.

The missed turns throughout my life didn't become aparent until much later. I came during a session of deep meditation led by Acharya Jonas early in 2026. It was during that meditation that I met the little boy sitting by the concrete yard. That's when I asked him what he wanted. That's when I felt my own self standing up on that bench and going join the boys play before another whistle came.
