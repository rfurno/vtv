# The Lost Match
## All I Wanted Was to Play Futebol

I have a picture from when I was nine. Not a photograph. A picture I can still relive.
Recess. A bench at the edge of the concrete floor at school improvised as soccer field. A small notebook on my knee. The other boys already choosing sides.

I heard my mother's friend telling me I was going to be special, or I had decided that she had. I would make important discoveries. I do not remember the sentence. I remember the job. I don't remember my mother's face in that conversation but I remember the double-job of wanting to make her proud.

So I stayed on the bench and tried to come up with the ideia genial, the idea that would prove it. I held the pencil and waited for it to arrive.

I remember scribbling things that would hardly fit together. Words looking for logic.

Logic that was not at the grasp of a nine-year old boy.

It did not. The whistle did. Recess was over. I had not played.


What I see now is plainer than the job I gave that boy. The important thing was not to become him. The important thing was to know I was not special.
