## Note 1: The Hook
The most important form in Ving Tsun has zero kicks, zero steps, and zero 5-finger-secret-killer techniques.

It's called Siu Nim Tau — "The Little Idea." You stand almost still and cycle one arm. That's it.

For hours.

Bruce Lee would be bored to death. But that's the point.

## Note 2: The Etymology Play
Kung Fu (功夫) literally means "skillful work + raw energy, wielded by a mature adult."

Not "badass fighter." Not "hero." Not "chosen one."

Just someone who showed up long enough to stop needing applause.

The character 夫 depicts a man with a hairpin — someone who's come of age. The ancients didn't romanticize mastery. They bureaucratized it. 🪮

## Note 5: The Challenge
Try this right now:

Stand still. Eyes closed. Visualize your palms turning forward. Then let your hands follow — slowly. One full minute for that tiny movement.

If your mind starts narrating, correcting, or rushing — notice that. That's the "doer" at work.

If the hand eventually moves by itself while the mind just watches — notice that too. That's the shift.

The difference between those two things is the entire point of both Ving Tsun and Vedanta.
