# The Little Idea: When Kung Fu Becomes Sadhana

Siu Nim Tau — the “Little Idea” form — was the first thing my Sifu taught me in Ving Tsun. For hours I stood almost completely still, cycling one arm, then the other, then both. No kicks. No steps. No trace of the explosive Bruce Lee sequences I had fantasized about.
What began as a brutal test of patience slowly revealed something deeper. First came the ability to visualize the movement before any muscle fired. Then came the quiet realization that this was purification: burning away the childish desire to leap through the air and knock an opponent out the way the movies had promised. The fantasy had to die.
In the etymology of Kung Fu (功夫), this is exactly what Kung (功) points to — the consummation of a feat through disciplined effort, the marriage of skillful work (工) and raw energy (力). Fu (夫) adds the mature, heroic character who wields that skill with moral clarity instead of ego. Not a flashy fighter. A mature practitioner.
This is where Kung Fu and Sadhana meet.
