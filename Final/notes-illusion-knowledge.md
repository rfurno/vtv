# Illusion of Knowledge

AI can produce impressive summaries by mashing up the entire internet. It often sounds authoritative, but it’s still just recombining existing information.

What I’ve been receiving in the long-term Vedanta course with Acharya Jonas feels different. It’s not the accumulation of interesting facts or clever comparisons between traditions.

It’s slower. It’s less about being “led to see” and more about being invited to experiment for myself — to try, to question, to challenge, to disagree, and eventually to settle into something that feels like *mine*. Not because someone explained it well, but because I’ve brought it into my daily life and tested it there.

For example, when I asked about the rope/snake analogy, an AI gave me a clean-sounding answer: “The rope is less real than the snake, but still not ultimately real — only Brahman is.” It sounded sophisticated, but it completely missed what I was actually learning in class.

What I learned — and I emphasize the *I* — was that the whole framework of “reality,” “lesser reality,” and “ultimate reality” was being taken apart. There was no solid conceptual ground left to stand on. I wasn’t collecting knowledge. I was being guided to see through the very way I was constructing it.

That difference feels important to me. I can honestly say I’ve been guilty of over-relying on data instead of lived experience. With AI everywhere, it’s easy to mistake fast, polished information for the kind of understanding that only comes through living transmission.
