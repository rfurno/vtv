# The Escape I Built by Hand, Sixteen and Already Leaving
## If the world rises and sets with the mind, why do the same unfinished patterns keep coming back?

The Vedanta class said the universe is part of the mind, that the world rises and sets with it.

That idea didn't catch me by surprise because I had already been exposed to a similar idea from reading Bernardo Kastrup's book "Why Materialism Is Baloney?". What surprised me and was harder to digest was the next claim: that continuity itself is held by karma.

Whether continuity through karma is the question I need right now is harder. I am not sure it is the piece that matters for where I am.

But the idea that a change in mind produces an outer one is puzzling. I'm slowly reframing the idea of cause and effect. I'm also less willing to stop at the convenient claim that it is only coincidence.

I borrow from an earlier class where I heard – or so I decided I heard – that time is not linear and therefore there is a simpler view that the inner and outer unfold together as if they both interacted with each other.

That's a question I haven't had a chance to ask Acharya Jonas.

## My Private Island That Never Was

As a teenager, senior high school, I'd spend hours creating a world in my mind. An island. I'd imagine bit by bit, even how the sun would set. I don't remember exactly every detail I've imagined but I remember well the feeling: I was not happy outside and was looking for a perfect world inside where I could be happy. I remember wanting to share that imaginary island with another person. I don't remember if I did or not, but I do remember I had trouble approaching girls in college to the point I had to see a therapist.

What was I running away from?

Why did this memory come back while I was writing about a world that rises and sets with the mind? I was not trying to remember it. I was trying to understand whether an inner change and an outer change arrive together or in sequence. The island was an attempt to make an inner world complete enough that the outer one would not matter. It never was.

## Some Things Take Longer Than a Life

It seems odd that as early as 16 I already wanted to run away from this world into a better one. I didn't think of taking my life away. I wanted my life to be something else.

And although it only took a few therapy sessions to get the guts to approach girls in college, it is taking decades to realize and cure the same reaching-for-withdrawal.

Is this the point where karma explains it? If a lifetime is too short to clear what is already here, then the continuity that karma is said to hold is not an abstract claim. It is the reason the same unfinished patterns can still be present. What I still do not see is whether an inner shift and the outer conditions that meet it ever appear together, or whether one always trails the other by years.

## Simple Explanations Are Useful

Imagine using quantum mechanics to answer the question of how long it would take for a car to move from point A to point B. It's pointless. Newtonian mechanics does a much better job at it.

Cause and effect, and coincidence, can still be useful tools. But I sense there's a more powerful explanation that would unveil how the world unfolds inside and outside simultaneously. I can't imagine a world in which action and reaction are not taking place in the same breath.

Perhaps it's just the mind — this sequence of thoughts I call mind — articulating what has already taken place, and that articulation gives the impression of a time sequence.

Maybe I will ask him if inner and outer worlds are overlapping states — if I can manage to ask it without sounding clever. In the meantime the mind has already filed the question under “later”.
