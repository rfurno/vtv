"We Were Never Cleaning the School. The School Was Cleaning Us."
That Japanese souji ritual — kids sweeping and wiping floors every afternoon, no janitors — isn’t really about clean tiles. The school was quietly shaping the kids through small, daily care.
When I started Ving Tsun, they told me to clean the Mo Kwoon and wash Sifu’s car. It felt bolted on. I did it, but didn’t internalize it.
Years later the practice landed with Kung Fu Life: helping classmates as the older kung fu brother, learning attentiveness when Sigung silently refuses poorly made tea. Small acts that reshape you.
Sadhana hits the same spot: mantras, japa mala, showing up early for class, helping others. It displaces the dust — news binges and doomscrolling — one disciplined day at a time.
Japan built this into school life. I finally built it into mine. The floor (or the form, or the mind) gets cleaner as a side effect.
